# Clinical Study Report Excerpt - Study ONC-401-202

**Confidential.** Abbreviated CSR, Section 11 (Efficacy) and Section 12 (Safety).
Investigational product: Oncorafenib (ONC-401). Data cutoff: 15 May 2026.

## 11.1 Analysis Populations (p.18 §11.1)

A total of **108 patients** with FGFR2-fusion-positive intrahepatic
cholangiocarcinoma received at least one dose of Oncorafenib and comprise the safety
population. **103 patients** were response-evaluable (measurable disease at baseline
with at least one post-baseline tumor assessment) and comprise the primary efficacy
population. Median age was 59 years (range 34-81); 61% were female; the median number
of prior systemic lines was 1 (range 1-4). Median follow-up at cutoff was 17.8 months.

## 11.2 Primary Endpoint - Objective Response Rate (p.19 §11.2)

The confirmed objective response rate by independent central review per RECIST v1.1
was **42%** (43 of 103 patients; 95% CI 32-52), comprising 3 complete responses and
40 partial responses. The lower bound of the confidence interval excluded the
pre-specified null value of 15%; the ORR was statistically significant with a
**p-value of <0.001** against the null hypothesis of ORR ≤ 15%. The disease control
rate (complete response, partial response, or stable disease) was **82%** (84 of 103;
95% CI 73-89).

## 11.3 Key Secondary Efficacy Endpoints (p.20 §11.3)

Among responders, the **median duration of response was 9.7 months** (95% CI
7.6-17.0). By independent central review, the **median progression-free survival was
8.9 months** (95% CI 6.7-11.4). The **median overall survival was 21.3 months** (95%
CI 15.8-not estimable), with OS data 46% mature at cutoff.

## 12.1 Overall Safety Summary (p.24 §12.1)

Treatment-emergent adverse events were reported in 99% of the 108 treated patients;
most were grade 1-2. The most common any-grade TEAEs were **hyperphosphatemia (78%)**,
alopecia (45%), stomatitis (42%), diarrhea (38%), fatigue (35%), dry mouth (32%), and
dysgeusia (30%). Grade ≥3 TEAEs occurring in at least 5% of patients were
hyperphosphatemia (12%), stomatitis (6%), and palmar-plantar erythrodysesthesia (5%).

## 12.2 Adverse Events of Special Interest (p.25 §12.2)

Hyperphosphatemia, an on-target class effect of FGFR inhibition, was managed with
dietary modification, phosphate binders, and dose modification; no patient
discontinued for hyperphosphatemia. **Serous retinal detachment** was reported in
**8%** of patients (grade ≥3 in <1%) and was reversible with dose interruption.
Dose interruptions occurred in 58% of patients and dose reductions in 42%. Permanent
discontinuation due to a TEAE occurred in **9%** of patients. There were no
treatment-related deaths.
