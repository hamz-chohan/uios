# Investigator's Brochure Excerpt - Oncorafenib (ONC-401)

**Confidential.** Investigator's Brochure, Edition 5, Sections 4-6. For investigator
reference only.

## 4.1 Mechanism of Action (p.7 §4.1)

Oncorafenib (ONC-401) is an orally bioavailable, ATP-competitive, selective
small-molecule inhibitor of the fibroblast growth factor receptor (FGFR) family. It
inhibits FGFR1 through FGFR4 with the **greatest potency against FGFR2**
(biochemical IC50 approximately 1.2 nM), and shows selectivity over a broad kinase
panel. By binding the ATP pocket of the constitutively active FGFR2 fusion protein,
Oncorafenib blocks receptor autophosphorylation and downstream RAS-MAPK and PI3K-AKT
signaling, arresting proliferation of FGFR2-fusion-driven tumor cells. The compound's
relative activity against FGFR1 is the mechanistic basis of the on-target
hyperphosphatemia observed clinically.

## 4.2 Nonclinical Pharmacology (p.8 §4.2)

In cholangiocarcinoma xenograft models bearing FGFR2 fusions (including FGFR2-BICC1),
once-daily oral Oncorafenib produced dose-dependent tumor growth inhibition and
frank regression at exposures consistent with the clinical 20 mg dose. No meaningful
activity was seen in FGFR2 wild-type models, supporting a biomarker-defined
population. Repeat-dose toxicology in rat and dog identified reversible effects on
phosphate homeostasis, nail, and corneal epithelium consistent with FGFR pathway
modulation.

## 5.1 Clinical Pharmacokinetics (p.10 §5.1)

Following oral administration, Oncorafenib is absorbed with a median Tmax of 2-4
hours and a mean terminal half-life of approximately 17 hours, supporting once-daily
dosing. Exposure is approximately dose-proportional across the studied range, and
steady state is reached by day 8. A high-fat meal did not produce a clinically
meaningful change in exposure; the drug may be taken with or without food.
Oncorafenib is metabolized primarily by CYP3A4; strong CYP3A4 inhibitors and
inducers should be avoided. No dose adjustment is warranted for mild hepatic or renal
impairment.

## 6.1 Pooled Safety Experience (p.13 §6.1)

Across the Phase 1 and Phase 2 program, **145 patients** with advanced FGFR-altered
solid tumors have received Oncorafenib. The pooled safety profile is consistent with
the pivotal Phase 2 study ONC-401-202. The most frequently reported adverse reaction
is **hyperphosphatemia**, an expected on-target pharmacodynamic effect that is
predictable, monitorable, and manageable with dietary phosphate restriction,
phosphate-lowering therapy, and dose modification. Other common events include
alopecia, stomatitis, nail changes, dry mouth, dysgeusia, and diarrhea.

## 6.2 Risks of Special Interest (p.14 §6.2)

**Ocular toxicity**, including reversible serous retinal detachment, is an identified
class risk of FGFR inhibitors and was observed in approximately 8% of patients in the
pivotal study. Ophthalmologic assessment at baseline and for new visual symptoms is
recommended. **Hyperphosphatemia** with prolonged, uncontrolled elevation carries a
theoretical risk of soft-tissue mineralization and should be managed per protocol.
Embryo-fetal toxicity is anticipated based on mechanism; effective contraception is
advised. No treatment-related deaths have been reported in the program to date.
